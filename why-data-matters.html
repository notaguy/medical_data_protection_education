Module 01

Why does my data matter?

Medical data is hard to change, easy to misuse, and worth more on the black market than a card number alone.

A medical file often ties together your name, date of birth, address, national ID, insurance details, diagnoses, medications, hospital stays, and sometimes information about your family. You can replace a leaked password. You cannot replace a lifetime of diagnoses.

Criminals want this package because it supports long-running fraud: fake billing to insurers, stolen medical identity, prescription fraud, and blackmail. A sensitive diagnosis — mental health, HIV, infertility, addiction — can be used to pressure someone into silence.

Health details also make scams more convincing. A fake message that names a real clinic, a recent test, or a specific doctor is harder to dismiss than a generic “your parcel could not be delivered” email.

For fraud networks, a full record is efficient. One clinic, lab, fitness app, or hospital incident can expose thousands of people at once, without stitching together separate leaks.

People also give data away without meaning to: a photo of a discharge letter, a “free health check” form, a patient portal left open, a wellness app that shares with advertisers. The harm is the same whether the data was stolen or handed over in a hurry.
