Module 02

Who can see my medical data?

Legitimate access is limited and purpose-bound. Unintended access often starts with a click, a photo, or a third-party app.

People involved in your care may need your data to treat you: doctors, nurses, laboratories, pharmacies, and, where relevant, your insurer or public health system. That access should be limited to what the job requires.

Employers, schools, and researchers should not see your clinical record by default. If they ask, ask why, what will be stored, and whether you can refuse or provide a narrower certificate instead of a full file.

Health and wellness apps — steps, sleep, symptoms, period tracking, home tests — often sit outside the hospital. They may share data with analytics firms, advertisers, or “research partners.” Read who the app names as a recipient before you grant contacts, photos, or microphone access.

Anyone you send a document to can keep a copy: family chats, Facebook groups, second-opinion forums, cloud backups. Deleting your post does not delete their screenshot.

Criminals see data when they phish a portal password, impersonate a clinic, break into a provider system, or buy a leaked database. They do not need to be in the building. A convincing SMS or a reused password is enough.

If you are unsure who has a copy, start with: the clinic’s official portal, your insurer, apps you installed, and every place you photographed or forwarded a result.

This list is a public-education map, not a legal inventory of every processor. It will be aligned with literature and official guidance.
