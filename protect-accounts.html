Module 04

Protect your accounts and data

You do not need to become a security expert. You need official channels, unique logins, and less sharing than feels convenient.

Open the hospital, lab, or insurer site yourself. Do not use a link or attachment in an email or SMS that claims “your results are ready” or “confirm your appointment.” If the message feels urgent, call the number on the organisation’s official website.

Use a unique, long password for health portals and turn on two-factor authentication when it exists. Never reuse your email password there. On a shared phone or computer, sign out — closing the tab is not the same as logging out.

Do not photograph or post documents that show your name, national ID, insurance number, appointment codes, or full results. If you need advice, describe the situation without identifiers. Crop out wristbands and screens in hospital photos.

On public Wi-Fi, skip the patient portal. Use mobile data. Check that the address is https and that the organisation name in the URL is correct.

Refuse app permissions that are unrelated to the feature (contacts, gallery, microphone for a step counter). Turn off optional “research” and social sharing. Treat free genetic tests and “risk assessments” that ask for your real name and conditions as a data handover.

Talk with family about what not to forward on chat apps: results, referrals, insurance cards. Children and older relatives are frequent targets of fake “hospital” messages.

If you suspect fraud: change passwords, tell the clinic, and, where relevant, the police or data protection authority. Keep the message and sender as evidence. Do not open the attachment.
